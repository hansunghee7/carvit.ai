How we chose the Carvit logo, one step at a time.

2026-10-03 · Jitu · Version 1.0

Tech Report 4: we followed the order professional designers use, and every step that changed the result left a number or a picture. The first four candidates all failed. The one we kept was chiseled by measurement, not by taste.

See the logo mapSee what changed the mark

Key findings

  • We researched how designers make logos before drawing anything, then followed that order: one strategy sentence, a map of competitors, black-and-white and 16px tests, optical correction.
  • All four first candidates failed. Round C shapes are a crowded place (Clerk, Coursera, Coda, CircleCI, Convex, C++), and any detail that carries meaning disappears at 16px.
  • Placing candidates among 40 real logos at the same size and colour showed look-alikes immediately, which no discussion had.
  • Two reviews by AI agents set the numbers (width to height 0.88, strokes 12 and 11, opening 38%). We then chose one mark for every size, and kept the design-tool selection handles only as a hero animation.

Why we started.

“Every step that changed the mark left a number or a picture. The ones that left only an opinion did not survive.”

The Carvit home page had a placeholder letter C where the app icon belongs. The founder said a logo would finish the page.

Our first move was to draw four candidates straight away. The founder stopped it with one instruction: first find out how professionals make logos. That changed the order of everything after it, and without it one of the four failed candidates would have shipped.

Research came before drawing.

We asked a search-connected model one batch of questions: how agencies run the process, the practical rules for logos of developer and AI tools, and what a one-person company should watch for when an AI does the work. Then we opened every source it returned. In the first round 6 of 14 links opened, and in the second about half of 17. A claim whose source did not open was not used. For example, a story about how one well-known tool made its logo is not in this report for that reason.

What held up: make the strategy and the competitor map before sketching; a logo must be recognisable in black and white and survive at 16px; correct for how the eye sees (a circle looks smaller than a square of the same height); show one concept or at most two or three; and keep vector originals, usage rules, and a trademark search.

One sentence of strategy.

“Carve our standard into what AI builds.” The name Carvit is “carve” plus “it”. The founder corrected the first reading: Carvit is not just a developer AI tool. It connects design (the UX guide) with AI development, so the competitor map had to include design tools too.

Four candidates, four failures.

The first four candidates were drawn before the research. Each failed for a reason that a test, not an opinion, revealed.

1

A. Chiseled bold C

At 40px the chiseled end was invisible, so it became a plain C, the same family as Clerk and Coursera.

2

B. Section sign

Everyone read it as an “S”. It also overlaps with Storybook.

3

C. Quote dot

The dot vanished at 16px, and at 40px the mark read as a “G”.

4

D. Chisel lines

At 16px the lines turned into a grey lump.

The lesson: round C shapes are a full room (Clerk, Coursera, Coda, CircleCI, Convex, C++), and a mark that hangs its meaning on a small detail loses it at 16px.

The map: 40 real logos.

We put the candidates among real logos at the same size and the same black. Twenty-four are development and AI tools, and sixteen are design and UX tools; the second group was added after the founder pointed out that Carvit sits between design and development. Look-alikes appear at once.

The numbers that changed the mark.

Round 2 had three candidates. A logo-expert agent, read-only, looked at the map and the pixel tests and gave scores and numbers. The first candidate scored 7, the chiseled block 6, the faceted C 5 and was dropped.

CandidateRound 2 scoreDesign-tool review
Chiseled C7Distinctiveness 7, meaning 4: nothing in the design row overlaps, but “connects design and development” is invisible.
Chiseled block6Buried among black rounded squares (Abstract, Adobe XD, InVision, LottieFiles). Kept for the app icon only.
Selected C(new in round 3)Distinctiveness 7, meaning 8 at 40px and up. Five elements, and thin lines vanish at 24px, so weak as a trademark.
Faceted C5Reads as low-poly, close to Cursor’s polyhedron. Dropped.

Table 1. How the three or four candidates scored in the two expert reviews.

The reviews also returned the measurements that shaped the final mark, in a 64 by 64 frame:

  • Proportion: frame x8 to 54, y6 to 58 (width to height 0.88). The first version was narrow and leaned left.
  • Strokes: vertical 12, horizontal 11. The horizontal had to be about 8% thinner to look equally thick.
  • Opening: end marks at y22 and y42, an opening of 38%. The first version opened too wide and read as a bracket.
  • End marks at 30° from horizontal, with the right shoulder cut reduced to 7 so the end does not thin out at 32px.
  • Optical shift: the open side looks lighter, so the shape sits one unit to the right in its frame.
  • 16px has its own drawing on a pixel grid (height 10, stroke 2), not a shrunk copy.

The decision, and where it is used.

Five contexts were shown on one sheet: the hero icon, the header at 24px, the browser tab at 16 and 32px, the app icon, and the share image. The founder chose option 1: one mark at every size. The selection handles of a design tool remain only as a short animation on the home page and in the share image, because the story they tell, “the standard you chose in design gets carved into what AI builds”, works as motion but weakens as a trademark.

From start to live on carvit.ai it took one day. The original files are on the brand page.

What we did not do.

  • We have not finished a trademark search. The word mark “CARVIT” comes first; a C-shaped mark has many earlier marks, so we will see those results before deciding what to file.
  • Both expert reviews were done by AI agents, one acting as a logo designer and one from a design-tool angle. No human designer outside the team reviewed the mark.
  • We did not test the mark with users or measure recognition. The 16px and 40px tests were done by looking.
